Glucose–HbA1c Distribution Mapper

Map the glucose distribution of a population to its expected HbA1c distribution with the law HbA1c(u) = eα × Glucose(u)β, where u is the percentile. Take the exponent β from your own data or from a reference population (Le Van Thinh Hospital, Sa Dec General Hospital or NHANES 2021–2023), and set the position with your own HbA1c values. Every result comes with a bootstrap 95% interval and a sample-size rating.

How to read the results

The law links two distributions at the same percentile: the glucose value at the 30th percentile of a population maps to the HbA1c value at its 30th percentile. Results are population percentiles and shares. They are not estimates of any individual patient’s HbA1c.

In the reference populations the exponent β was similar (0.58–0.68), whereas the position differed between Vietnam and the United States by about 15 mg/dL of glucose at HbA1c 7%. Borrowing a reference β and anchoring the position with your own HbA1c values (about 100 recommended) is therefore usually better than using a reference position unchanged.

Indicative sample sizes from resampling: about 150 values per test to estimate the glucose equivalent of HbA1c 7% within ±5 mg/dL, about 600 to estimate β within ±0.05, and about 100 HbA1c values to anchor the position. The law may be biased in insulin-treated patients.

Reference data are embedded only as aggregated summaries (49 percentiles and 500 bootstrap estimates of α and β per population); no individual records are included. Source: Tran HQ, Vo KT, Tran LQP, Tran NTH, Nguyen CH, Le HTK, Lam TK. A glucose–HbA1c distribution law for monitoring glycaemic control in diabetes (manuscript submitted).
